\section{K-harmonic regression and F-test}
\label{sec:kharmonic_test}
%% ====================================================================

The K-harmonic extension in the main text is a linear regression on
periodic basis functions. For period $T=24$ and angular frequency
$\omega_0 = 2\pi/T$, the model is
\begin{equation}
y(t) = \mu + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \big[ a_k \cos(k \omega_0 t) + b_k \sin(k \omega_0 t) \big] + \varepsilon,
\label{eq:supp_kharmonic}
\end{equation}
with $2K + 1$ free parameters: an intercept and $2K$ harmonic
coefficients. The single-harmonic cosinor test is recovered at $K=1$;
the two-harmonic test used in the main text sets $K=2$.

We test the null hypothesis of no rhythm,
\begin{equation}
H_0:\; a_1 = b_1 = a_2 = b_2 = \cdots = a_K = b_K = 0,
\end{equation}
against the alternative that at least one $(a_k, b_k) \neq (0, 0)$.
Stacking the $N$ observations into $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and
building the design matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_K = \big[ \mathbf{1},\; \cos(\omega_0 \mathbf{t}),\; \sin(\omega_0 \mathbf{t}),\; \ldots,\; \cos(K \omega_0 \mathbf{t}),\; \sin(K \omega_0 \mathbf{t}) \big]
\in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (2K+1)},
\end{equation}
the nested-model F-statistic is
\begin{equation}
F = \frac{( \mathrm{SSE}_0 - \mathrm{SSE}_K ) / (2K)}
        {\mathrm{SSE}_K / (N - 2K - 1)},
\label{eq:supp_f_stat}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SSE}_0 = \sum_i (y_i - \bar y)^2$ and
$\mathrm{SSE}_K = \mathbf{y}^\top (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{H}_K) \mathbf{y}$
with $\mathbf{H}_K = \mathbf{X}_K (\mathbf{X}_K^\top \mathbf{X}_K)^{-1} \mathbf{X}_K^\top$.
Under Gaussian errors and the null,
\begin{equation}
F \mid H_0 \sim F(2K,\; N - 2K - 1) \quad \text{(exact, finite-sample)}.
\label{eq:supp_null_dist}
\end{equation}
No asymptotics, boundary correction, or empirical calibration is
required, because \eqref{eq:supp_kharmonic} is fully linear in
$(\mu, a_k, b_k)$ and every parameter is identifiable under both $H_0$
(where they equal zero) and $H_1$.

In practice, \eqref{eq:supp_kharmonic} is fitted through the
\textsc{LimoRhyde} framework \citep{Singer2019LimoRhyde}. The software returns
the empirical-Bayes moderated F-statistic by default (for $G \geq 50$)
and falls back to the exact F-statistic at small $G$ ($G < 50$).
The moderated F-statistic is used throughout the main paper.

\section{Type-I error validation of the K-harmonic F-test}
\label{sec:typeI}
%% ====================================================================

Type-I error of the K-harmonic F-test was validated by simulating
null expression matrices drawn from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ independent of
collection time on an equispaced active grid at
$n \in \{12, 24, 48, 72, 144\}$ with
$N_{\mathrm{sim}} = 2000$ Monte Carlo replicates per cell. The
empirical rejection rate at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ was compared to
the Monte Carlo $\pm 3$\,SE tolerance band $[0.035, 0.065]$. Both the
empirical-Bayes moderated and exact $F$ paths were tested at
$K = 1$ and $K = 2$ for all $N$; the exact-$F$ path was tested at
$K = 3$ for $n \geq 12$ where the design is identifiable.
The reproducible validation script and saved log are included with the
software release.

Across the calibration grid (40 detector $\times$ $N$ cells), empirical
type-I error fell in $[0.043, 0.057]$, well within the
$[0.035, 0.065]$ tolerance band, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov uniformity
tests on the simulated null $p$-values did not reject uniformity at
$p_{KS} > 0.01$ in every cell. Representative cells: $K = 1$ exact-$F$ at $n = 48$,
$T_I = 0.056$, $p_{KS} = 0.012$; $K = 2$ eBayes $n = 72$,
$T_I = 0.051$, $p_{KS} = 0.978$; $K = 3$ exact-$F$ $n = 144$,
$T_I = 0.051$, $p_{KS} = 0.385$. Identifiability-threshold behaviour
($K = 2$ at $B = 4$) is enforced by the dispatch-level rank-check
warning emitted by the runner, which guards against rank-deficient
design matrices before any test is computed.

\section{Empirical harmonic-order sensitivity}
\label{sec:k_sweep}
%% ====================================================================

Increasing $K$ expands the signal space but also consumes two
additional degrees of freedom per added harmonic. We evaluated this
trade-off on the baboon KIM pilot (GSE98965, \citep{Mure2018baboon}),
an active design with $B=12$ sampling times. This grid satisfies the
identifiability threshold for $K=3$ ($B \geq 2K+1 = 7$). Power was
estimated over $N \in \{24, 48, 72, 144\}$ with
$N_{\mathrm{sim}} = 30$ outer replicates per cell.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
\centering
\small
\caption{Marginal rhythmicity power on baboon KIM at $B = 12$.
$K = 1$ is the single-harmonic cosinor F-test; $K \geq 2$ uses the
K-harmonic F-test. Power was computed at FDR $0.05$ over
$N_{\mathrm{sim}} = 30$ outer replicates.}
\label{tab:k_sweep_kim}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$N$  & $K = 1$ & $K = 2$ & $K = 3$ \\
\midrule
$24$ & $0.262$ & $\mathbf{0.427}$ & $0.380$ \\
$48$ & $0.749$ & $0.856$ & $0.855$ \\
$72$ & $0.906$ & $0.952$ & $0.953$ \\
$144$ & $0.984$ & $0.992$ & $0.993$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

At $N = 24$, $K = 2$ outperforms $K = 3$ by $4.7\%$. This indicates that
the third harmonic does not compensate for the additional degrees of
freedom at small sample size. At $N \geq 48$, the $K = 2$ and $K = 3$
curves coincide within Monte Carlo noise ($<0.2\%$ at every $N$), so
the third harmonic provides no practical gain in this setting. The
$K = 1$ test is consistently less powerful in this pilot. Together,
these results support $K=2$ as a practical default for this analysis
setting, while leaving the optimal harmonic order dependent on waveform
shape, sample size, and the planned sampling grid.

\section{Simulation algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}
%% ====================================================================

Algorithm~S1 describes the data-generation procedure for a single
simulation replicate of the two-group differential setting at sample
size $N$. The single-cohort case is recovered by setting the
differential proportions
$\pi_{\mathrm{DR}} = \pi_{\mathrm{DP}} = \pi_{\mathrm{DM}} = 0$ and
the second-group parameter modifications to no-ops.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\small
\hrule\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Algorithm S1}: Two-group circadian simulation \vspace{2pt}
\hrule\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Input}: number of genes $G$, sample size $N$, type
probabilities $\pi_R, \pi_{\mathrm{DR}}, \pi_{\mathrm{DP}}, \pi_{\mathrm{DM}}$,
empirical pilot distributions $\hat{F}_\mu, \hat{F}_{A,\sigma}, \hat{F}_\phi$
and empirical TOD distribution $\hat{F}_{\mathrm{TOD}}$, design type
(active or passive), phase shift $\Delta\phi$, mesor shift $\Delta\mu$,
seed $s$.\\
\textbf{Output}: expression matrices $\mathbf{Y}_1, \mathbf{Y}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times N}$
and the ground-truth type assignment $\mathbf{T}$.
\vspace{4pt}

\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Seed} $s$. For $g = 1, \ldots, G$, draw baseline
parameters: $\mu_g \sim \hat{F}_\mu$, $(A_g, \sigma_g) \sim \hat{F}_{A,\sigma}$
(joint draw preserves the pilot's amplitude-noise dependence), and
$\phi_g \sim \hat{F}_\phi$.

\item \textbf{Assign gene types} $T_g \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ by
sampling from the categorical with probabilities
$P(T_g = 0) = 1 - \pi_R - \pi_{\mathrm{DR}} - \pi_{\mathrm{DP}} - \pi_{\mathrm{DM}}$,
$P(T_g = 1) = \pi_R$,
$P(T_g = 2) = P(T_g = 3) = \pi_{\mathrm{DR}} / 2$,
$P(T_g = 4) = \pi_{\mathrm{DP}}$,
$P(T_g = 5) = \pi_{\mathrm{DM}}$.

\item \textbf{Initialise group parameters} as
$(\mu_{g,k}, A_{g,k}, \sigma_{g,k}, \phi_{g,k}) = (\mu_g, A_g, \sigma_g, \phi_g)$
for $k \in \{1, 2\}$, then modify according to $T_g$:
$T_g = 0$: $A_{g,1} = A_{g,2} = 0$ (arrhythmic);
$T_g = 1$: no modification (rhythmic in both);
$T_g = 2$: $A_{g,2} = 0$ (rhythmic in group 1 only);
$T_g = 3$: $A_{g,1} = 0$ (rhythmic in group 2 only);
$T_g = 4$: $\phi_{g,2} = (\phi_{g,1} + \Delta\phi) \bmod 24$;
$T_g = 5$: $\mu_{g,2} = \mu_{g,1} + \Delta\mu$.

\item \textbf{Reseed} $s + 7919 N$. For each group $k$ and each
$i = 1, \ldots, N$, draw collection times $t_{k,i}$: from
$\hat{F}_{\mathrm{TOD}}$ for passive designs, or from the equispaced
active grid otherwise. This second seeding step makes time-of-day
draws sample-size-specific while preserving the per-gene biological
parameters across $N$.

\item \textbf{Generate expression}. For each gene $g$, group $k$, and
sample $i$,
\[
y_{g,k,i} = \mu_{g,k} + A_{g,k} \cos\!\big( \omega_0 (t_{k,i} - \phi_{g,k}) \big)
+ \varepsilon_{g,k,i},
\qquad \varepsilon_{g,k,i} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\big(0, \sigma_{g,k}^2 \big),
\]
with $\omega_0 = 2\pi / 24$.

\item \textbf{Record ground truth} $\mathbf{T} = (T_1, \ldots, T_G)$
along with per-gene effect sizes $r_{g,k} = A_{g,k} / \sigma_{g,k}$,
phase differences, and mesor differences.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{2pt}\hrule
\end{figure}

The two-stage seeding (steps 1 and 4) holds the gene-type assignment
and baseline parameters fixed across sample sizes within a simulation
replicate while reseeding only the $N$-dependent quantities (collection
times and residual noise). This isolates the effect of $N$ on power
from incidental variation in the gene-level biological configuration.

\section{Design matrix properties and B-invariance under cosinor truth}
\label{sec:b_invariance}
%% ====================================================================

This section provides the mathematical derivation underlying the
$B$-invariance result used in the main text active-design analysis. Under
balanced equally spaced sampling with $B \geq 3$ distinct time points
and $m$ replicates per time point ($N = B m$), the harmonic-coefficient
block of the cosinor design matrix is diagonal and proportional to $N$,
which removes any dependence of the noncentrality on $B$.

\subsection{Setup}

Let $t_b = (b - 1) \cdot 24 / B$ for $b = 1, \ldots, B$ denote the
distinct sampling times of a balanced equally spaced active design over
one $24$-hour cycle, and $\theta_b = \omega_0 t_b = 2 \pi (b - 1) / B$
the corresponding phases on the unit circle. The cosinor design matrix
for one gene is $X_{ij} = f(t_i)_j$ with
$f(t) = [1, \cos(\omega_0 t), \sin(\omega_0 t)]^\top$.

\subsection{Off-diagonal terms vanish for $B \geq 3$}

The off-diagonal sums
$\sum_b \cos(\theta_b), \sum_b \sin(\theta_b),$ and
$\sum_b \sin(\theta_b) \cos(\theta_b)$
are finite geometric sums of complex exponentials and all vanish for
$B \geq 3$ by the standard finite-sum identity (the denominators
$\sin(\pi / B)$ and $\sin(2 \pi / B)$ are non-zero for $B \geq 3$). At
$B = 2$ the sine column of $X$ is identically zero, so the cosinor
design is rank-deficient; we therefore impose $B \geq 3$ throughout.

\subsection{Diagonal terms equal $B / 2$}

Using $\cos^2 \theta = (1 + \cos 2\theta) / 2$ and the same finite
trigonometric sum identity to make $\sum_b \cos(4 \pi (b-1) / B) = 0$
for $B \geq 3$, we obtain
$\sum_b \cos^2 \theta_b = \sum_b \sin^2 \theta_b = B / 2$. With
balanced replication $m_b \equiv m$ and $N = B m$, the design
information matrix takes the diagonal form
\begin{equation}
X^\top X = \mathrm{diag}\!\big( N,\; N / 2,\; N / 2 \big),
\qquad B \geq 3.
\label{eq:supp_xtx_balanced}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Noncentrality and B-invariance}

Under cosinor truth $y_{g,i} = \mu_g + A_g \cos(\omega_0 t_i - \omega_0 \phi_g) + \varepsilon_{g,i}$,
the harmonic-coefficient OLS estimator has Fisher information block
$(N / 2) I_2$ by~\eqref{eq:supp_xtx_balanced}. The noncentrality
parameter of the F-test for $H_0: A_g = 0$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
\lambda_g = \frac{N}{2} \cdot \frac{A_g^2}{\sigma_g^2} = \frac{N\, r_g^2}{2},
\qquad r_g = A_g / \sigma_g,
\label{eq:supp_ncp_cosinor}
\end{equation}
which depends on $N$ and the per-gene effect size $r_g$ but not on $B$.
This is the design factor $d(\phi) = 1 / 2$ uniformly in $\phi$ used in
the main text. Identical reasoning at each harmonic block of the
K-harmonic regression \eqref{eq:supp_kharmonic} extends the result to
$K \geq 2$ above the identifiability threshold $B \geq 2K + 1$,
yielding the $B$-invariance of the K-harmonic noncentrality used in
the active-design results of the main text.

\section{Design factor under passive sampling}
\label{sec:design_factor}
%% ====================================================================

For passive designs, the per-gene design factor depends on the
empirical TOD distribution rather than on a chosen grid. Let
$\hat{F}_{\mathrm{TOD}}$ denote the empirical distribution of collection
times across the planned cohort (or its bootstrap-resampled equivalent
in Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap_impl}). For a planned sample size $N$,
draw $t_{i} \sim \hat{F}_{\mathrm{TOD}}$ independently for
$i = 1, \ldots, N$; the conditional design factor is then
\begin{equation}
d(\phi_g) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \cos^2\!\big( \omega_0 (t_i - \phi_g) \big)
- \left[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \cos\!\big( \omega_0 (t_i - \phi_g) \big) \right]^2,
\label{eq:supp_d_passive}
\end{equation}
which is the conditional version of the CircaPower design factor
\citep{zong2023circapower}. Averaging $d(\phi_g)$ over the per-gene
phase distribution gives the marginal design efficiency loss relative
to the active-design value of $1/2$. For the post-mortem brain pilots
used in the main text (Putamen-SCZ, GTEx), the marginal
$d$ is approximately $0.20$ to $0.30$; the exact value depends on how
concentrated the empirical TOD distribution is. The active vs.\ passive design
factor difference quantifies the efficiency advantage of active
designs (factor of about $2$) at matched $N$ and $r$.

\section{Bootstrap implementation}
\label{sec:bootstrap_impl}
%% ====================================================================

The outer subject bootstrap of Section~2.3 propagates pilot
sampling variability into the projected power curve. Algorithm~S2
summarises the implementation, which is exposed as a single
end-to-end entry point in the \textsc{SCP} framework.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\small
\hrule\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Algorithm S2}: Single-cohort outer-bootstrap power \vspace{2pt}
\hrule\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Input}: pilot expression matrix $\mathbf{Y}_{\mathrm{pilot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times n_{\mathrm{pilot}}}$,
pilot collection times $\mathbf{t}_{\mathrm{pilot}}$, sample-size grid
$\{N_1, \ldots, N_J\}$, outer-resample count $B_{\mathrm{out}}$,
inner-simulation count $N_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{in}}$, the screening
threshold $\alpha_{\mathrm{pilot}}$, and FDR level $\alpha$.\\
\textbf{Output}: $(B_{\mathrm{out}} \times J)$ matrix of bootstrap
power replicates and pointwise $95\%$ CI bands.
\vspace{4pt}

\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item For $b = 1, \ldots, B_{\mathrm{out}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Resample subjects.} Draw $n_{\mathrm{pilot}}$ subject
indices with replacement from $\{1, \ldots, n_{\mathrm{pilot}}\}$ to
form $\mathbf{Y}^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times n_{\mathrm{pilot}}}$
and the matched time vector $\mathbf{t}^{(b)}$.

\item \textbf{Re-estimate pilot summary.} Refit the pilot summary on
$(\mathbf{Y}^{(b)}, \mathbf{t}^{(b)})$ to obtain
$\hat{\Psi}^{(b)} = ( \hat{F}_\mu^{(b)}, \hat{F}_{A,\sigma}^{(b)}, \hat{F}_\phi^{(b)}, \hat{F}_{\mathrm{TOD}}^{(b)}, \hat{\pi}_R^{(b)} )$.

\item \textbf{Inner simulation.} For each $j$ and each
$s = 1, \ldots, N_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{in}}$, simulate a fresh
gene-by-sample matrix from $\hat{\Psi}^{(b)}$ at sample size $N_j$ via
the procedure of Algorithm~S1, apply the chosen rhythmicity detector
(cosinor $F$-test or K-harmonic F-test), apply BH adjustment at
$\alpha$, and record the rhythmicity discovery rate.

\item \textbf{Aggregate.} Average the discovery rate across the
$N_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{in}}$ inner replicates to obtain the bootstrap
power estimate $\widehat{\mathrm{Power}}^{(b)}(N_j)$.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Summarise across outer resamples.} Compute the bootstrap
mean
$\bar{\mathrm{Power}}(N_j) = B_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1} \sum_b \widehat{\mathrm{Power}}^{(b)}(N_j)$
and the pointwise $2.5$th and $97.5$th percentiles of
$\widehat{\mathrm{Power}}^{(b)}(N_j)$ across $b$.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{2pt}\hrule
\end{figure}

Default production settings are $B_{\mathrm{out}} = 50$,
$N_{\mathrm{sim}}^{\mathrm{in}} = 25$, and
$\alpha_{\mathrm{pilot}} = 0.01$, used for the bootstrap panels in
the main text.
